Dear Editors,

Please consider for publication our manuscript "Heterogeneous
supervision and evaluation validity in cancer assertion extraction",
a Research and Applications contribution.

Cancer informatics increasingly relies on automated extraction of
drug, gene, variant, and disease assertions from the biomedical
literature to support clinical knowledge-base curation. Practitioners
typically select among candidate models by their performance on
public NLP benchmarks (BioRED, BC5CDR), implicitly assuming that
benchmark performance is a faithful proxy for downstream
knowledge-base utility. This assumption has not, to our knowledge,
been tested directly at scale on a curator-validated oncology
knowledge base. We test it through a pre-registered factorial study
of 310 training runs evaluated against 162 curator-validated CIViC
assertions, introducing three KB-audit metrics and a variance
decomposition that contrasts benchmark- and KB-explained variance
under the same design lever.

The result contradicts the implicit benchmark-as-proxy assumption.
Training schedule explained more variance in KB surfacing than in
benchmark F1 (configuration-arm variance ratio 0.21, 95% CI
[0.03, 0.99]), opposite to our pre-registered direction; half of
benchmark-tied configurations reversed their KB ordering. Without
the pre-registration the reversed direction would have been
difficult to distinguish from selective reporting. The implication
for clinical-NLP pipelines is concrete: selection on benchmark F1
alone risks selecting configurations whose downstream KB yield is
not maximised, and KB-anchored evaluation should be a routine
component of model-selection protocols.

The main text is approximately 3,000 words with two tables and five
figures; the supplementary material contains full numerical results,
the falsification audit chain for the parameter-efficient
fine-tuning arm, and the pre-registered statistical analysis plan.
All training and analysis code, the pre-registration document, and
the amendment log are openly available at
https://github.com/MRCIEU/assertion_extraction. The work has not
been published or submitted elsewhere, and we declare no competing
interests.

AI disclosure (per JAMIA policy): A large language model (Claude
Opus 4.7, temperature = 0, deterministic decoding) was used as the
